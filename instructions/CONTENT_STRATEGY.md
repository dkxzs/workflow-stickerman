# CONTENT STRATEGY — YOUTUBE STICKMAN EXPLAINER

## 1. CHANNEL OBJECTIVE

Build a long-term YouTube channel targeting English-speaking audiences through original 2D Stickman Explainer videos.
The channel should focus on topics that combine:

- Genuine audience interest.
- Strong storytelling potential.
- Clear visual explanation opportunities via 2D Stickman animation.
- Sustainable content production.
- Long-term monetization potential.

The objective is not to chase temporary viral trends.

## 2. STRATEGY STATUS

**IMPORTANT:** The final content niche has **NOT** been selected.
All proposed niches and content categories must be treated as research hypotheses until validated.
Do not automatically assume that Psychology, Self-Improvement, Survival, History, Science, Economics, or any other category is the final direction.
The Content Researcher and Market Analyst must investigate and compare multiple opportunities in `01_research.md` before a niche is approved by the user.

## 3. TARGET AUDIENCE

Primary audience:

- English-speaking viewers.
- United States as the main initial market.
- International English-speaking audiences (UK, Canada, Australia, etc.) as secondary markets.

The exact age range and audience profile should be determined through competitor research and actual channel analytics when available.

## 4. NICHE VALIDATION CRITERIA

Evaluate potential niches using these five criteria:

### 4.1 Audience demand

- Are people actively watching this type of content?
- Are there successful channels in the category?
- Are individual videos receiving meaningful views?
- Are there signs of continuing evergreen interest rather than one-time viral spikes?

### 4.2 Competition

- How many established creators serve this audience?
- Are smaller or newer channels gaining traction?
- Are there underserved topics or fresh angles?
- Is the market dominated by a few large channels?
- Can a new creator realistically differentiate the content?
- _Rule:_ Do not label a niche as "low competition" simply because there are few search results.

### 4.3 Content & Visual opportunities

- Can the category generate at least 30+ distinct video ideas?
- Are there compelling questions that viewers want answered?
- Can the subject be explained clearly through **2D Stickman characters, simple props, and minimalist diagrams**?
- _Filter:_ Reject topics that rely heavily on real-world live-action footage, complex 3D simulations, or copyrighted movie/news clips to make sense.

### 4.4 Production feasibility

- Can the videos be produced cleanly with the available Python rendering engine and hardware?
- Can factual research be completed reliably using verifiable sources?
- Can the 2D Stickman visual style be maintained consistently across episodes?
- Is the production time reasonable?

### 4.5 Monetization potential

Consider:

- Advertiser demand and brand safety (avoiding sensitive/demonetized subjects).
- Audience geography (tier-1 English-speaking markets).
- Video length and watch time potential.
- Sponsorship and affiliate opportunities where relevant.
- _Rule:_ Never assume a category guarantees a high RPM without evidence.

## 5. COMPETITOR RESEARCH

For each promising niche, investigate and document in `01_research.md`:

1. At least 3–5 relevant YouTube channels.
2. Their most successful videos (outliers).
3. Recent uploads (last 3–6 months) and their performance.
4. Their average content format and duration.
5. Their storytelling techniques and hook styles.
6. Their visual presentation and pacing.
7. Their audience engagement (comment patterns, recurring questions).
8. Potential content gaps a new channel can fill.

Separate large established channels from smaller emerging channels. Prioritize identifying opportunities that a new channel can realistically compete for. Never copy competitor scripts, titles, or visual identities.

## 6. TOPIC SELECTION

A good topic should have:

- A clear, specific, and intriguing question.
- A strong curiosity gap.
- A compelling, logical explanation.
- Enough substance for a complete 6–8+ minute video without filler.
- Reliable, verifiable sources.
- Strong Stickman visual storytelling potential.
- A title that communicates value quickly.

Prefer specific questions over broad generic subjects:

- _Broad (Avoid):_ Human Survival.
- _Specific (Prefer):_ How Did Ancient Humans Survive Freezing Winters Without Modern Heating?
  _(Note: This example is illustrative only, not an approved topic.)_

## 7. CONTENT DIFFERENTIATION

The channel should differentiate itself through:

- Clear, structured, and entertaining explanations.
- Strong narrative progression.
- Original everyday examples and clever comparisons.
- Expressive 2D Stickman characters with distinct personalities.
- Occasional clever humor and light sarcasm.
- Sharp visual timing and pacing.
- Reliable, well-checked information.

Do not depend entirely on animation style as the only source of originality.

## 8. CONTENT PILLARS

Content pillars must be selected **after** niche validation and user approval.
Once a niche is approved:

- Define 3–5 main content pillars.
- Identify subtopics under each pillar.
- Create a shortlist of potential video ideas.
- Identify which ideas are suitable for initial testing.

Do not build a large content calendar before the niche is validated.

## 9. INITIAL VIDEO STRATEGY

After selecting the niche:

1. Identify a shortlist of promising topics and research the top candidate thoroughly (`01_research.md`).
2. **Gate 1 (Research Approval):** Submit niche and topic research for user approval.
3. Develop the Concept Angle, 3 title options, and Thumbnail concept (`02-concept.md`).
4. **Gate 2 (Concept Approval):** Submit the concept for user approval, then record the finalized selection under `## Approved Concept` inside `projects/<project_id>/01_research.md`.
5. Write and review the full script based on the Approved Concept (`02_script.md`).
6. **Gate 3 (Script Approval):** Submit the script for user approval before storyboarding.
7. Produce the storyboard, audio, visuals, and final video (`output/final_video.mp4`).
8. Run QA (`qa_report.md`) and prepare publishing metadata (`output/metadata.txt`) for final user review.

The initial videos should test both topic interest and the channel's presentation style. Do not interpret a single video's performance as definitive proof that a niche will succeed or fail.

## 10. PERFORMANCE EVALUATION

When channel analytics become available, evaluate:

- Impressions and Click-through rate (CTR).
- Average view duration (AVD) and Average percentage viewed (APV).
- Audience retention curve (especially the first 30 seconds and drop-off spikes).
- Returning viewers and Subscribers gained.
- Traffic sources and Geographic distribution.

Use these metrics to diagnose bottlenecks and improve future scripts/visuals. Do not judge success using view count alone.

## 11. MONETIZATION STRATEGY

The primary long-term goal is sustainable YouTube monetization.
Potential future revenue sources may include:

- YouTube AdSense revenue.
- Brand sponsorships.
- Affiliate marketing.
- Relevant digital products.

Never compromise audience trust for short-term revenue. Always verify current YouTube Partner Program eligibility requirements and monetization policies.

## 12. STRATEGY REVIEW

Review the channel strategy after a meaningful batch of published videos (e.g., 5–10 uploads).
Determine:

- Which topics attract and retain viewers best?
- Which formats are most efficient to produce?
- Which content gaps remain unexploited?
- Whether the current niche should be maintained, narrowed, or adjusted.

Major strategic changes should be based on data and evidence rather than a single disappointing upload.
