# Research & Concept Dossier — `video-002`

## 1. Topic Overview & Historical / Medical Facts (Verified)

- **Subject**: Randy Gardner's 1964 sleep deprivation science fair experiment in San Diego, California.
- **Participants**:
  - **Randy Gardner** (17-year-old high school student at Point Loma High School).
  - **Bruce McAllister** and **Joe Marciano Jr.** (two classmates who stayed awake in shifts to monitor Randy and keep him from nodding off).
  - **Dr. William C. Dement** (Stanford University sleep researcher who joined the experiment on Day 4 after reading about it in the newspaper).
  - **Lt. Cmdr. John J. Ross** (U.S. Navy Medical Neuropsychiatric Research Unit in San Diego, who logged clinical neurological observations day by day).
- **Duration**: **264.4 hours (11 days and 25 minutes)** without sleep, from December 28, 1963 to January 8, 1964, breaking the previous record of 260 hours held by Honolulu DJ Tom Rounds.
- **No Stimulants**: Gardner used no caffeine, no amphetamines, and no stimulant drugs of any kind.

### Verified Chronological Clinical Symptoms (Ross, 1965; Dement, 1999)
1. **Days 1–2 (Hours 24–48)**:
   - Difficulty focusing eyes (asthenopia); by Day 2 he stopped watching television because his eyes hurt and refused to focus.
   - Mild impairment in **stereognosis** (inability to identify objects strictly by touch with eyes closed).
   - Heightened sensitivity to light and mild mood irritability.
2. **Days 3–5 (Hours 72–120)**:
   - **Ataxia** (loss of motor coordination) and difficulty repeating simple tongue twisters.
   - Nausea and acute sensitivity to smells (complained bitterly about the smell of baking donuts).
   - **Microsleeps**: brief 2-to-5-second intrusions of theta brainwaves while eyes remain physically open.
   - **First Visual Hallucinations (Day 4)**: Mistook a street sign for a person standing on the sidewalk; imagined himself as **Paul Lowe**, the famous San Diego Chargers running back, insisting angrily that his friends not question his athletic ability.
   - Memory lapses and vivid daydream-like episodes where walls seemed to move or paths appeared inside his living room.
3. **Days 6–8 (Hours 144–192)**:
   - **Speech deterioration**: Slowed, slurred speech, monotone delivery, and frequent failure to finish sentences because his working memory dropped the thought mid-phrase.
   - **Paranoia and Irritability**: Suspicion toward observers, sudden mood swings when pushed to perform cognitive tests.
   - Severe tremor in hands and fingers, drooping eyelids, and expressionless facial mask.
4. **Days 9–11 (Hours 216–264)**:
   - Fragmented thinking: during the **Serial Sevens test** on Day 11 (counting backward from 100 by 7s: 93, 86, 79, 72, 65...), he stopped completely at 65. When asked why he stopped, he replied that he had forgotten what he was doing.
   - **The Pinball / Ping-Pong Paradox**: Despite severe cognitive and linguistic breakdown during passive paper tests, when Dr. Dement took him to play pinball and physical games, adrenaline briefly overrode the fatigue and Randy won repeatedly.
   - Final press conference on January 8, 1964: spoke coherently under the bright lights before being taken to the U.S. Naval Hospital in San Diego.
5. **The Recovery Twist & Decades-Later Aftermath**:
   - **First recovery sleep**: Slept for **14 hours and 40 minutes** (waking at 8:40 PM on January 8), then slept 10.5 hours the next night.
   - **EEG findings**: His brain showed a massive **REM sleep rebound** (percentage of REM sleep surged while light sleep decreased) to repair neural synapses.
   - **Long-term twist**: For decades, Randy seemed completely fine. However, in his 60s, he developed **severe, debilitating chronic insomnia** for over a decade—describing it as "karmic payback" for pushing his brain past its biological circuit breakers at age 17.
   - **Guinness World Records decision**: Stopped monitoring sleep deprivation records in 1997 because the medical risk of fatal microsleeps, psychosis, and autonomic failure is too dangerous.
